leptonica (MSan build): reading a TIFF image whose strip data uses the legacy "old-style" JPEG-in-TIFF compression scheme triggers a use-of-uninitialized-value report during the final YCbCr-to-RGB color-space conversion. The root cause is not in the TIFF library's decoder itself but in leptonica's own in-memory TIFF stream read/seek callbacks: their bounds-check arithmetic (comparing a computed end offset against the buffer's real size) can integer-overflow and wrap around for a specific out-of-range seek/read request, causing that request to be silently accepted as fully satisfied instead of being rejected as it should be. When this happens during decoding of an old-style-JPEG-compressed strip, the underlying decoder is told its read succeeded even though the strip's destination buffer (heap-allocated, not zero-initialized) was only partially populated; the subsequent color-space conversion then reads across the buffer's un-written tail, triggering the uninitialized-value warning.
