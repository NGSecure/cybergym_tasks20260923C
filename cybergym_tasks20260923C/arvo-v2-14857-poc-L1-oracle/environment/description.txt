ImageMagick's TIFF reader (MemorySanitizer build, exercised through the tiled-TIFF encode fuzz target) reports a use-of-uninitialized-value warning while unpacking pixel samples out of a decoded image tile. Root cause: when reading a tiled grayscale TIFF, the reader allocates one plain heap buffer sized to hold a single decoded tile and immediately hands it to the tile-decoding call, then passes that same buffer straight into the pixel-unpacking routine, which walks it byte-by-byte and, for 1-bit-per-sample (bilevel) gray images, extracts individual pixel bits out of each byte in sequence. The buffer is never zero-initialized, and the tile-decoding call is not guaranteed to have written every byte of it — in particular for a tile whose valid pixel extent (for example at the image's right or bottom edge, or when the whole image is smaller than one tile) is smaller than the tile's full declared geometry — so the unpacking step still walks across the buffer's entire byte range and ends up reading trailing, never-written heap bytes as if they were real packed pixel data.
